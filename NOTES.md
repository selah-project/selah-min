# CATATAN — tarjamahan Minang Selah

File iko mamacik **ayaik-ayaik nan alah disantuah dek tangan atau dek kaputusan** — supayo pambaco nan mamareso ayaik apo sajo dapek mancaliak apokah ayaik itu dibuek dek mesin, dibaiki, atau dipacik dek aturan, jo baa dek. Iko juo mamacik kaputusan tantang kato-kato tatantu, ayaik-ayaik nan sulik, jo tanyo-tanyo nan masih tabuka jo manunggu talingo urang Minang.

Isinyo dalam Baso Inggirih, dek karano catatan-catatan iko manyabuik bantuak kato Ibrani, jo Baso Inggirih adolah baso karajo nan dipakai basamo di sado tarjamahan Selah. Ibraninyo ado di satiok catatan. Catatan dibaiki, indak dikapuih diam-diam — sajarah repositori iko adolah bagian dari kasaksiannyo.

Baco [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md) untuak caro naskah iko dibuek, jo [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) sabalun mambaiki.

## §1 · Verses written by hand

None. Every verse in this repository was rendered by machine under the
written rules (`docs/methodology/translation-discipline/min.md` in the main
Selah repository). Where a verse was faulty, it was rendered again from its
Hebrew, not patched by hand — except for the mechanical repairs listed in §3.

## §2 · Words fixed by name while the work was under way

- **`Makrifat` forbidden in the Name's seat** · `יהוה` · Deut 6:21–24 came back
  with *Makrifat* (a Sufi word for mystical knowledge) where the Hebrew has
  the Name, in a single batch written early in the work, and nowhere else.
  The word was added to the forbidden column of the Name table and to the
  checks; the four verses were rendered again and now carry **Yahweh**
  (Deut 6:21 · *tapi Yahweh mandapek kito kalua dari Mitsrayim*) · 2026-10-04.
- **Supplied words in Minangkabau only** · the instructions for supplied words
  were restated with no English examples: copula, article and pronoun are
  supplied **in Minangkabau**; an article-less language supplies no article;
  never ⟨ה⟩; inside ⟨…⟩ only Minangkabau, ⟨את⟩ the one exception. Measured by
  file time, English or ⟨ה⟩ inside brackets fell from 0.61 % of verses before
  the change to 0.05 % after · 2026-10-04.

## §3 · Repairs made after the first pass

Each of these was found by the written checks and the verse rendered again
from its Hebrew, unless the entry says otherwise.

- **Jer 34:19** · a corrupted Hebrew surface: one word row read `יהוה` where the
  Hebrew is `יהודה` (Judah); the flowing verse was right. The surface was
  restored from the Hebrew text. Rows now read `שרי · יהודה · ושרי · ירושלם` ·
  2026-10-04.
- **Jer 22:6** · an empty gloss in the Name's row and stray debris in the
  flowing verse · rendered again.
- **1 Sam 23:11–12** · the flowing verse carried Yahweh but one row's gloss was
  empty · rendered again.
- **Jer 32:17–20** · came back half in English (*You have made ⟨את⟩ the
  heavens…*) · rendered again.
- **A broken marker `⟨את]]`** · Gen 37:29 and a few others · rendered again; the
  marker now reads ⟨את⟩.
- **Hebrew left in the flowing verse** · small clusters at Ps 119:157–160,
  Prov 7:22–24 and Dan 2:45–47 · rendered again.
- **Mojibake** · five verses carried a quote mark garbled through the wrong
  encoding (*maâ€™adokean*) · rendered again.
- **Restored, not re-rendered** · three verses whose new rendering lost the
  agreement between ⟨את⟩ in the flowing verse and ⟨את⟩ in the rows were
  restored from their first version, with the stray ⟨the⟩ / ⟨ה⟩ dropped
  (Minangkabau has no article). Hebrew surfaces that had drifted were restored
  from the Hebrew text in a mechanical pass and re-checked.
- **A second check of the rows** (2026-10-06) · every verse whose number of
  rows did not match its Hebrew words, whose content was hollow, or which
  carried English inside ⟨…⟩, was removed and rendered again. That re-check is
  in progress as this file is written.

## §4 · Lawful — do not correct

- **Exod 1:15 · `dukun bayi`** for `מילדת`, the midwives Shifrah and Puah ·
  *dukun bayi* is the ordinary Minangkabau word for a midwife. The rule against
  *dukun* is for `נביא` (a prophet is **nabi**, never *dukun*, the diviner
  Deuteronomy 18 forbids).
- **`tuhan-tuhan` for the gods of the nations** · Judg 16:23 (*Dagon, tuhan-tuhan
  mareka*), Deut 32:16 (*Tuhan-tuhan asiang*) · lower-case *tuhan* is lawful for
  other gods and idols; the capital at the head of a sentence is only the
  sentence. The rule forbids *Tuhan* in the Name's seat, not the word.
- **`tuan`, `tuan ambo`** for `אדני` / `אדון` addressed to a human · lawful; *Adonai*
  is for `אדני` addressed to Elohim.
- **An empty gloss on a particle** · e.g. `ויהי` in Gen 22:1 · a single empty gloss
  on a narrative particle with no Minangkabau counterpart is lawful; a hollow
  verse is not.
- **`Allah` in a note field** · some files carry a `rationale` field with the
  phrase *Bait Allah* (1 Kgs 8:28, 1 Chr 22:12). That field is a working note,
  not the rendering; the flowing verse and rows of both read **Elohim**.

## §5 · Known faults — open

These verses still need to be rendered again. They stand here so that no one
reads them as settled.

- **Isa 62:5** · `אלהיך` glossed and rendered **`Allah awak`** — *Allah* in
  Elohim's seat, which the Name table forbids. The same verse carries a casing
  fault (*pANGANTIN*) and an underscore joining two words (*manari_atas*, with
  Indonesian *atas* for Minangkabau *ateh*).
- **Isa 9:20** · *Dengan sado iko…* — Indonesian *dengan* at the head of the
  last clause, where Minangkabau says *jo*.
- **⟨את⟩ in the flowing verse differs from ⟨את⟩ in the rows** · kept in their
  newer rendering, to be read: 1 Kgs 18:18 · 1 Kgs 8:20 · 2 Kgs 10:6 ·
  2 Kgs 12:8 · Deut 6:24 · Exod 10:11 · Exod 29:7 · Gen 45:13 · Gen 5:2 ·
  Jer 39:5 · Jer 51:12 · Lev 10:19 · Mic 7:14 · Prov 17:21 · Ps 106:17 ·
  Ps 136:11 · Ps 44:21.
- **1 Chr 11:22** · `אריאל` glossed *Ari-El (urang singo-Tuhan)* — a parenthetical
  gloss with *Tuhan* where the Hebrew has `אל`. Should read **El**, and the
  parenthesis is outside the discipline.

## §6 · Open questions — awaiting a Minangkabau ear

- **`dabu`** for `עפר` (dust) · or another word? **`padang`** for `חרב` (sword) ·
  or *pedang*? *Padang* is also a field; context decides. **`ati`** for `לב` ·
  or *hati*? **`kapatang`** for `תמול` (yesterday).
- **Names** · Hebrew sound (*Avraham*, *Yitskhak*, *Dawid*) against the familiar
  forms; and **`Shabat` · `Mishkan` · `Shaddai`** with *sh*, beside **`syikal`** with
  *sy* — which spelling does a Minangkabau reader expect?
- **`dan` / `Dan`** · the corpus uses *dan* often, beside *jo*, e.g. Isa 53:5
  *Dan inyo dilukoi dek pambahuoan kito…* (`והוא מחלל מפשענו`). Is *dan* natural
  Minangkabau here, or Indonesian? The rules list *jo* for *and*.
- **`mareka`** · Judg 16:23 *tuhan-tuhan mareka* · a Minangkabau-spelled form of
  Indonesian *mereka*; the rules give **urang tu**.
- **`Sanak` addressed to Yahweh** · Ps 23:4 *karano Sanak basamo ambo*; Jer 32:17
  *Aduhai Adonai Yahweh, caliak, Sanak alah majadian…* · the Hebrew has the
  plain second person `אתה`. Is *Sanak* (kinsman, the polite *you*) the right
  register for addressing Elohim, or should it be *Angkau* / *Kau*, as
  1 Kgs 8:28 has (*Jo palingkok Kau ka doa hamba-Mu*)?
